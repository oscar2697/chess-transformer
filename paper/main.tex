\title{Searchless Chess Transformer: Attention-Based Policy and Value Learning}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}We investigate whether a pure Transformer, trained only on high-ELO games and deprived of any explicit search, can predict strong chess moves (policy) and evaluate positions (value) through attention alone. On a 570k-position elite dataset, a masked cross-entropy objective lifts top-1 move prediction from chance level to 15.5\% (top-3 36\%) on held-out positions. The value head, in contrast, is characterized as a constant-predictor collapse: under game-result targets, loss reweighting ($\times$3) and head-specific LR ($\times$5) leave the MSE at the label-variance floor; under engine-evaluated targets (Lichess public evaluations DB), the MSE drops to 0.038 but the predicted-value standard deviation is 0.000 and Pearson correlation with Stockfish is 0.0 --- the model simply outputs the target mean. We show why MSE alone is misleading for value heads and identify gradient competition with the policy objective as the likely cause. All experiments were orchestrated by a five-agent LLM pipeline whose decisions are logged for reproducibility. We release the full pipeline as an open research scaffold for searchless chess.\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Inspired by the DeepMind view that rich representations can be learned without hand-crafted heuristics~\cite{Silver2017_1712_01815}, we ask how far a searchless Transformer can go in classical chess: no MCTS, no alpha-beta, only attention over a FEN token sequence feeding policy and value heads. This work contributes (i) a from-scratch policy/value Transformer evaluated with legal-move masking, (ii) an analysis of what legal-move masking contributes over naive cross-entropy, (iii) a CLS-attention interpretability protocol for tactical motifs (RQ3), and (iv) an LLM graph-agent pipeline that makes every experimental decision auditable. We deliberately report intermediate, honest results: the goal is to document the trajectory of a research scaffold, not to claim playing strength.
\section{Related Work}
\cite{Vaswani2017_1706_03762} Attention Is All You Need -- The dominant sequence transduction models are based on complex recurrent or convolutional neural networks in an encoder-decoder configuration. The best performing models also connect the encoder and decoder through an attention mechanism. We propose a new simple network architecture, the Transformer, based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely.

\section{Method} We tokenize the FEN string character-level (with reserved \texttt{[CLS]}/\texttt{[SEP]} and padding indices) and feed an 8-layer, 512-dim Transformer encoder. Two heads share the CLS representation: a policy head classifying 1{,}968 UCI moves (legal moves masked at inference and, in the masked run, in the loss) and a value head with tanh output in $[-1,1]$ trained against game results. Training uses AdamW with cosine schedule, AMP, per-epoch checkpointing with resume, and 570k/30k train/val positions from the Lichess Elite database (three months, ELO\,$>$\,2000, deduplicated).
\subsection{Graph Agent Methodology} Five LLM agents (researcher, data\_engineer, trainer, evaluator, writer) orchestrate the pipeline via \texttt{src/graph\_agents/llm\_agents.py}. Each agent combines a role prompt with a deterministic tool (the corresponding node); with \texttt{LLM\_PROVIDER} + API key it reasons via LLM, otherwise it runs deterministically. Every decision is logged to \texttt{experiments/agent\_log.jsonl} for reproducibility.

Figure~\ref{fig:loss_curve} reports the training and validation loss trajectories for the four configurations. The unmasked baseline plateaued almost immediately (7.698$\to$7.623 train, validation oscillating 7.61--8.37), extracting no generalizable signal. The masked-v1 run trained at a much lower scale (total loss near 3.81, CE $\approx$3.03, validation top-1 0.150--0.165). The value-v1 run (initialized from masked-v1, loss weight $\times$3, head LR $\times$5) left the game-result MSE essentially unchanged (0.770$\to$0.781). The engine-v1 run replaced the supervision itself: we joined our positions against the Lichess public evaluations database (394M Stockfish-evaluated positions), matching 12.5\% of train (71k) and 18\% of val (5.4k); centipawn scores were mapped to $[-1,1]$ via a 400cp logistic, mates to $\pm1$. The engine-target MSE settles at 0.038 --- dramatically lower than 0.78, but for the wrong reason, as we show next. Each epoch required approximately 12 minutes (full dataset) or 1.5 minutes (matched subset).

We evaluated the best masked-v1 checkpoint on 200 held-out positions from val.jsonl using game-result value targets, obtaining a top-1 move accuracy of 15.5\%, top-3 of 36\%, and a value MSE of 0.719; the value-v1 checkpoint scores 14\%/31\%/0.729 on the same protocol. These figures are consistent with the training-time validation top-1 band (0.150--0.165), which validates the evaluation protocol. We note a methodological pitfall discovered during this project: an earlier evaluation run on a fresh machine silently scored a \emph{randomly initialized} model (3\% top-1) because the checkpoint directory was empty and the evaluator fell back to random weights. This artifact was eliminated by requiring the evaluator to resolve an existing checkpoint or fail loudly, and by logging the checkpoint path inside the results JSON --- an example of the auditability the agent pipeline provides.

\noindent\textbf{The constant-predictor collapse.} The engine-v1 evaluation exposes the true failure mode of the value head. Against engine targets (n=200 held-out, matched subset) it reports top-1 26\%, top-3 42.5\%, MSE 0.022 --- but a Pearson correlation with Stockfish of \textbf{0.0}. Direct inspection confirms why: on seven hand-picked positions spanning clearly winning, losing and balanced material (e.g., an extra queen for the opponent), the model outputs the same value ($+0.0321$) with a prediction standard deviation of \textbf{0.000}. The head has collapsed to predicting the target mean. The ``good'' MSE is a statistical artifact: engine targets of elite positions concentrate near zero (variance $\approx$0.03--0.04), so a constant predictor attains exactly that floor --- just as it attains 0.72 under game-result labels (variance $\approx$0.72). The two regimes were never different: MSE alone cannot distinguish a value head from a constant baseline; prediction variance and correlation can, and both are reported in our evaluation protocol from now on. We attribute the collapse to gradient competition: the cross-entropy term (scale $\approx$3.0) dominates the shared trunk's updates, while the tiny MSE gradients (residuals near zero once the mean is predicted) provide no escape signal; loss reweighting $\times$3 was insufficient, and the 71k matched subset is both small and biased toward popular, balanced positions. Remedies for future work: train the value head from scratch on engine targets with a much larger weight or a separate trunk, larger and debiased matched datasets, and the 8$\times$8 plane representation whose spatial structure should ease value extraction. (The engine-v1 policy numbers, 26\%/42.5\%, are not comparable to the 15.5\% figure: they are measured on the matched subset, which is biased toward frequently analyzed positions.)

\section{Attention Analysis}
The loss decomposition indicates that the policy objective was learnable under masking while the value objective was not: the cross-entropy component accounted for nearly all of the training signal, whereas the MSE component remained flat throughout. Validation top-1 accuracy oscillated without monotonic improvement, and the learning-rate schedule cycled between $10^{-3}$ and $0$; we interpret these curves as preliminary evidence of a model at the edge of a trainable regime rather than converged behavior.

\section{Conclusion}
The masked-CE objective is the single highest-impact design decision in this study: it moved policy prediction from chance to 15.5\% top-1 / 36\% top-3 on held-out elite positions, with held-out figures matching the training-time validation band. The value head proved resistant to every intervention: game-result targets (noisy), loss reweighting and head-specific LRs (no effect), and engine-evaluated targets (constant-predictor collapse with Pearson 0.0, unmasked only by reporting prediction variance and correlation alongside MSE). The value story of this paper is therefore a methodological one: (i) MSE floors track target variance and can fake learning; (ii) diagnosing the collapse required exactly the auditability the agent pipeline provides (logged checkpoints, loud evaluator failures, per-metric JSONs); (iii) the collapse points to gradient competition with the dominant policy objective as the mechanism, and to from-scratch value training, debiased matched data, and the spatial 8$\times$8 representation as the remedies. Attention has not crystallized into tactical focus either (entropy 4.0 vs.\ $\ln 64$), consistent with a trunk shaped almost exclusively by the policy gradient. Future work: (i) value-from-scratch on engine targets with variance/correlation reporting, (ii) scale toward the ChessBench regime~\cite{Ruoss2024_2402_04494}, (iii) reintroduce search as an evaluation-time comparison rather than a training crutch. The graph-agent pipeline itself is a contribution: every number in this paper is traceable to a logged, validated agent decision.
